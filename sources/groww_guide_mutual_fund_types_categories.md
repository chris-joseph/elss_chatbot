# Types and Categories of Mutual Funds

The Securities and Exchange Board of India (SEBI) categorizes mutual fund schemes into five broad groups to bring uniformity, transparency, and clear risk differentiation across all Asset Management Companies.

## 1. Equity Mutual Funds

Equity funds invest primarily in equity and equity-related securities of companies listed on stock exchanges. Under SEBI rules:
- **Large Cap Funds**: Must invest at least 80% of assets in the top 100 companies by full market capitalization.
- **Mid Cap Funds**: Must invest at least 65% in mid-sized companies ranked 101st to 250th by market cap.
- **Small Cap Funds**: Must invest at least 65% in companies ranked 251st and beyond by market cap.
- **Flexi Cap Funds**: Dynamic equity funds with minimum 65% equity allocation across large, mid, and small caps with complete flexibility for the fund manager.
- **Multi Cap Funds**: Must invest at least 25% each in large caps, mid caps, and small caps.
- **ELSS (Equity Linked Savings Scheme)**: Tax-saving equity funds with minimum 80% equity allocation, eligible for Section 80C tax deduction with a 3-year lock-in period.
- **Sectoral / Thematic Funds**: Must invest at least 80% in a single specific sector (like Banking, IT, Pharma, or Infrastructure).

## 2. Debt Mutual Funds

Debt funds invest in fixed-income instruments like Government Securities (G-Secs), Treasury Bills, Corporate Bonds, and Commercial Papers:
- **Overnight Funds**: Invest in securities having a maturity of 1 day.
- **Liquid Funds**: Invest in debt instruments with a maturity of up to 91 days.
- **Money Market Funds**: Invest in money market instruments with a maturity of up to 1 year.
- **Corporate Bond Funds**: Must invest at least 80% in highest-rated AA+ and AAA corporate bonds.
- **Gilt Funds**: Must invest at least 80% in sovereign government securities with zero credit default risk.

## 3. Hybrid Mutual Funds

Hybrid funds invest in a combination of both equity shares and debt securities to balance growth and capital protection:
- **Aggressive Hybrid Funds**: 65% to 80% equity, 20% to 35% debt.
- **Conservative Hybrid Funds**: 10% to 25% equity, 75% to 90% debt.
- **Balanced Advantage / Dynamic Asset Allocation Funds**: Dynamic allocation between equity and debt based on market valuation models.
- **Arbitrage Funds**: Minimum 65% equity allocation exploiting price differences between cash and derivative futures markets, treated as equity for tax purposes.

## 4. Solution-Oriented Schemes

Funds designed for specific life goals with mandatory lock-ins:
- **Retirement Funds**: Minimum 5-year lock-in period or until retirement age.
- **Children's Funds**: Minimum 5-year lock-in period or until child attains age of majority (18 years).

## 5. Other / Passive Schemes

- **Index Funds**: Passively managed funds that replicate the constituent stocks and weightages of a specific market index (e.g., NIFTY 50, Sensex).
- **ETFs (Exchange Traded Funds)**: Passively managed schemes traded live on stock exchanges during market hours.
- **Fund of Funds (FOF)**: Schemes that invest at least 95% of total assets in other mutual fund schemes.
