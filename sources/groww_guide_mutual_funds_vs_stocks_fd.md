# Mutual Funds vs Direct Stocks, Fixed Deposits, and PPF

Investors frequently compare mutual funds with direct equities (stocks), bank fixed deposits (FD), and public provident funds (PPF). Each instrument serves different risk-return profiles and investment objectives.

## Mutual Funds vs Direct Stocks

| Feature | Mutual Funds | Direct Stocks |
|---|---|---|
| **Diversification** | High; single scheme holds 30 to 100+ stocks across sectors | Low to moderate unless substantial capital is allocated across many companies |
| **Management** | Professional SEBI-registered fund managers and research analysts | Self-managed; investor must pick stocks, read financial statements, and track earnings |
| **Risk Profile** | Mitigates unsystematic company risk through portfolio diversification | High idiosyncratic risk; company bankruptcy or scandal directly impacts capital |
| **Minimum Capital** | Start with as little as ₹100 per month via SIP | Need enough capital to buy minimum share lot/price |
| **Trading Hours** | Orders execute at end-of-day NAV based on cut-off timings | Live real-time execution on stock exchanges during market hours |
| **Costs** | Total Expense Ratio (TER) deducted daily from NAV | Brokerage, exchange transaction charges, and demat charges per trade |

## Mutual Funds vs Fixed Deposits (FD)

| Feature | Mutual Funds | Fixed Deposits (FD) |
|---|---|---|
| **Returns** | Market-linked; no guaranteed return, but potential for higher long-term inflation-beating growth | Fixed guaranteed interest rate determined by the bank at deposit opening |
| **Safety / Guarantee** | Subject to market volatility; principal not guaranteed | Insured up to ₹5 Lakh per depositor per bank by DICGC |
| **Taxation** | Equity funds: 12.5% LTCG (>₹1.25L); Debt funds: slab rate | Interest income added annually to income and taxed at slab rate; TDS applies |
| **Liquidity** | Open-ended funds can be redeemed on any business day (T+1 or T+2 settlement) | Premature withdrawal penalty (typically 0.5%–1.0%) on early redemption |

## Mutual Funds (ELSS) vs Public Provident Fund (PPF)

- **Lock-in Period**: ELSS has a statutory lock-in period of **3 years**, which is the shortest among all Section 80C tax-saving investments. PPF has a strict statutory lock-in period of **15 years**.
- **Return Nature**: ELSS returns are market-linked equity returns; PPF returns are government-backed fixed interest rates declared quarterly.
- **Tax Benefit**: Both ELSS and PPF qualify for tax deductions up to ₹1.5 Lakh per financial year under Section 80C of the Income Tax Act (under the Old Tax Regime).
