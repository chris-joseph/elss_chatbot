# Direct Plans vs Regular Plans

## What is the Difference Between Direct and Regular Mutual Funds?

Every mutual fund scheme in India is offered by the Asset Management Company (AMC) in two distinct variants: **Direct Plan** and **Regular Plan**. Both variants invest in the exact same underlying portfolio of stocks or bonds and are managed by the same fund manager, but they differ significantly in costs and returns.

### Direct Plan
- **Direct Investment**: Investors purchase units directly through the AMC or through direct execution platforms like Groww without intermediaries.
- **Zero Distributor Commissions**: No brokerage or distributor trail commission is paid from the fund's corpus.
- **Lower Total Expense Ratio (TER)**: Because zero commission is paid, the annual expense ratio is 0.5% to 1.5% lower than regular plans.
- **Higher NAV and Returns**: Due to lower ongoing expenses, direct plans have a higher Net Asset Value (NAV) and yield consistently higher compounding returns over the long term.

### Regular Plan
- **Intermediary Purchase**: Investors buy units through a mutual fund distributor (MFD), broker, or bank relationship manager.
- **Ongoing Trail Commissions**: The AMC pays annual commissions out of the scheme corpus to the distributor for the entire duration the investment is held.
- **Higher Total Expense Ratio (TER)**: The expense ratio includes the distributor's ongoing commission.
- **Lower NAV and Returns**: The ongoing commission deduction reduces the net compounding returns earned by the investor.

## How to Identify Direct and Regular Plans

- Direct plans always have the word **"Direct"** explicitly printed in the scheme name (e.g., *Groww ELSS Tax Saver Fund - Direct Plan - Growth*).
- Regular plans omit the word "Direct" or include the word **"Regular"** in their official title.
- Direct plans and Regular plans have separate ISINs and publish different daily NAVs.

## Switching from Regular to Direct

Investors holding units in regular plans can switch their investments into direct plans:
- On Groww or through the AMC, investors can place a switch request from the regular plan to the direct plan of the same scheme.
- A switch is treated under Indian tax laws as a redemption from the regular plan followed by a fresh purchase into the direct plan.
- Applicable capital gains taxes and exit loads may apply if units have not completed their specified holding period.

## Direct Plan vs SIP: Key Differences & How They Work Together

A frequent point of confusion among mutual fund investors is comparing **Direct Plans** with **SIPs**. Direct Plans and SIPs are not competing or mutually exclusive options—they belong to two completely separate, orthogonal dimensions of mutual fund investing:

1. **Plan Variant (Direct vs Regular)**: Refers to **who you buy the fund from and how expense ratios are structured**. A Direct Plan eliminates intermediaries and distributor commissions, giving investors a lower Total Expense Ratio (TER) and higher Net Asset Value (NAV).
2. **Investment Mode (SIP vs Lump Sum)**: Refers to **how and when capital is invested**. A Systematic Investment Plan (SIP) is a disciplined method where a fixed amount (such as ₹500 or ₹1,000) is automatically invested at regular intervals (monthly or weekly) to benefit from rupee-cost averaging and compounding.
3. **They Work Together (Direct SIP)**: Investors do not have to choose between Direct and SIP. On direct mutual fund platforms like Groww, you can start a **Direct SIP**—combining the lower cost of a Direct Plan with the automated discipline of a monthly SIP. Alternatively, investors can also do a **Direct Lump Sum**, a **Regular SIP**, or a **Regular Lump Sum**.
