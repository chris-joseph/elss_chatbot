# Mutual Fund Taxation and Capital Gains Rules

Taxation on mutual funds depends on the asset class of the scheme (Equity or Debt) and the duration for which the units were held before redemption (holding period).

## Equity-Oriented Mutual Funds (Equity $\ge 65\%$)

Schemes that invest at least 65% of their total assets in domestic listed equity shares (including Large Cap, Mid Cap, Small Cap, Flexi Cap, and ELSS):

### 1. Short-Term Capital Gains (STCG)
- **Holding Period**: Units held for **12 months or less**.
- **Tax Rate**: Flat **20%** (amended in Finance Act 2024, previously 15%) plus applicable surcharge and 4% cess.

### 2. Long-Term Capital Gains (LTCG)
- **Holding Period**: Units held for **more than 12 months**.
- **Exemption Limit**: Capital gains up to **₹1.25 Lakh** per financial year across all equity investments are completely tax-exempt (increased from ₹1.0 Lakh).
- **Tax Rate**: Gains exceeding ₹1.25 Lakh are taxed at **12.5%** (amended in Finance Act 2024, previously 10%) without indexation benefits.

## Section 80C Tax Deduction for ELSS

- Equity Linked Savings Schemes (ELSS) are the only mutual fund category eligible for tax deduction under **Section 80C** of the Indian Income Tax Act (under the Old Tax Regime).
- Investors can claim deductions up to **₹1,50,000** per financial year, potentially saving up to ₹46,800 in taxes for individuals in the highest 30% tax bracket.
- ELSS schemes carry a mandatory statutory lock-in period of **3 years** from the date of allotment.

## Debt-Oriented Mutual Funds (Equity $< 35\%$)

For investments made on or after April 1, 2023, in debt mutual funds where equity exposure does not exceed 35%:
- All capital gains—regardless of holding period—are treated as short-term capital gains.
- Gains are added directly to the investor's total taxable income and taxed at the investor's applicable **income tax slab rate**.
- Indexation benefits are no longer available for debt funds purchased after April 1, 2023.

## Taxation on Switching and SIPs

- **Switching**: Switching units between schemes (e.g., from Regular to Direct, or from Debt to Equity) is legally treated as a redemption followed by a new purchase, attracting applicable capital gains taxes.
- **SIP Tax Calculation**: Each SIP installment is treated as an independent investment with its own distinct purchase date, NAV, and holding period. When redeeming, the "First-In, First-Out" (FIFO) accounting method is applied to determine the holding period for each unit.
